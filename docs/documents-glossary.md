# The Documents, Decoded

A home purchase generates a small forest of paperwork. Here is every major document, what it says, what it means, and what you should do when you receive it. When you get a new document, check it against this list first.

*Education only — read with your own attorney, agent, or lender for advice on your specific deal.*

## Documents you create or sign early

### Buyer representation agreement
- **What it is:** A contract between you and a buyer's agent, saying who represents you and how the agent gets paid.
- **What it means:** You are confirming that this person works **for you**, not the seller.
- **Watch for:** The term ("6 months" binds you longer than you might want), whether you can cancel early, and how compensation is described.

### Mortgage application (Form 1003)
- **What it is:** The official loan application — income, debts, assets, the property address once known.
- **What it means:** You are formally asking a lender for money, and they will verify everything.
- **Watch for:** Accuracy. Every number here gets matched against pay stubs, bank statements, and tax returns. Typos cause delays, not disasters — fix them fast.

### Purchase agreement / purchase and sale agreement (PSA)
- **What it is:** The legally binding contract for the house: price, dates, contingencies, and who pays for what.
- **What it means:** This is *the* deal. Everything else is paperwork flowing from it.
- **Watch for:** Contingency deadlines (inspection, financing, appraisal), the earnest money amount, and what happens if a date is missed. Ask your agent to walk you through deadlines in plain English before you sign, not after.

### Earnest money receipt
- **What it is:** Proof you deposited good-faith money (typically 1–3% of the price) toward the purchase.
- **What it means:** The seller takes you seriously. It is **not an extra fee** — it becomes part of your down payment if you close, and is generally returned if you back out under a valid contingency.

## Documents the lender sends you

### Loan Estimate (LE)
- **What it is:** A standardized 3-page summary of the loan you were offered: rate, monthly payment, estimated cash to close, and fees.
- **What it means:** This is your comparison tool when shopping lenders. Same price, same terms, received from two lenders? Put their LEs side by side.
- **Watch for:** Section G (origination charges) and Section B ("services you cannot shop for") versus Section C ("services you *can* shop for"). Fees in the shaded areas of page 2 are protected — they generally cannot increase at closing unless circumstances legally allowed them to change.

### Intent to proceed
- **What it is:** You telling your lender "yes, I want this loan quote."
- **What it means:** Nothing is charged and nothing is committed until you send this. You can walk away before it.
- **Watch for:** Nothing — just don't send it to a lender you're comparing against, only to the one you choose.

### Pre-approval letter
- **What it is:** A letter (often one page) stating roughly how much the lender is willing to lend you, *pending* verification and appraisal.
- **What it means:** Agents and sellers take you halfway seriously. It is **not** a loan. See [Pre-approval Without Panic](pre-approval.html).

### Closing Disclosure (CD)
- **What it is:** The final, legally required 5-page statement of your loan terms, monthly payment, fees, and exact cash to close. You must receive it at least **3 business days** before closing.
- **What it means:** This is the document proofreaders dream about. Compare it line-by-line with your Loan Estimate.
- **Watch for:** Cash to close (must match your wiring plan — see [Escrow & Closing Day](escrow-closing-day.html)), the loan term and rate on page 1, and any fee that grew from the LE. If numbers changed, ask "why?" — you are allowed, and the 3-day rule exists so you have time.

### Promissory note
- **What it is:** Your written promise to repay the loan, with the amount, rate, and term.
- **What it means:** This is the debt itself.
- **Watch for:** Whether the rate is fixed or adjustable, the exact term (360 months for 30-year), and any prepayment penalty language.

### Mortgage / deed of trust
- **What it is:** The security instrument — it lets the lender foreclose if you don't repay, and is recorded with the county.
- **What it means:** One document creates the debt (the note), the other lets the lender act on it (this one). Two signatures, two very different documents.
- **Watch for:** Whatever is mentioned about escrow accounts (taxes/insurance collected monthly) and adjustable-rate adjustment terms, if any.

## Documents the title company or attorney produces

### Title commitment ("title report")
- **What it is:** A document listing what the title insurer found about the property's ownership history, and any liens, easements, or restrictions ("requirements" and "exceptions").
- **What it means:** It tells you what comes *with* the property — debts attached to it (that must be paid off), rights of way, and rules recorded against it.
- **Watch for:** Old loans the seller hasn't paid off (they get cleared at closing), odd easements (utility access is normal; paths through your yard deserve a question),HOA or town covenants that affect what you can do with the property.

### Settlement statement / Closing Disclosure page 4–5
- **What it is:** The ledger of the whole transaction: who pays whom, and how much, item by item.
- **What it means:** Money going in (you) and money going out (to seller, county, agent, lender) all in one place.
- **Watch for:** Duplicate fees, misspelled names, and whether the earnest money is credited to you where you expect it.

### Title insurance policies
- **What it is:** Insurance against problems with ownership history — undiscovered heirs, forged deeds, recorded liens no one caught.
- **What it means:** The lender's policy protects the lender (required) and is yours to buy (the "owner's policy") protects you. Buying the owner's policy is common and generally recommended; in many places you get a discount if you buy both together.
- **Watch for:** Who paid for the owner's policy in your state's custom (it varies), and the exceptions listed on page 1 of the policy.

## Documents about the house itself

### Home inspection report
- **What it is:** The inspector's findings, often with photos, ranked by severity.
- **What it means:** A wish list, not a verdict. You'll use it to negotiate or to walk away during the inspection contingency.
- **Watch for:** Items the inspector says they *couldn't* inspect (roof under snow, sealed crawlspace) — "unknown" is different from "fine."

### Appraisal report
- **What it is:** The appraiser's opinion of the home's market value, ordered by your lender.
- **What it means:** It's for the **lender's** benefit: they don't want to lend $300,000 on a $260,000 house. See [Inspection vs. Appraisal](inspection-vs-appraisal.html).
- **Watch for:** "Comps" — recent nearby sales the appraiser used. If the value comes in low, you get to decide whether to renegotiate, pay the difference, or walk.

### Survey (sometimes)
- **What it is:** A drawing showing property boundaries and where improvements (fences, porches, driveways) sit relative to them.
- **What it means:** This is how you find out the neighbor's fence is actually on your side of the line — before you fight about it.
- **Watch for:** Encroachments (someone else's structure on your land) whether or not you plan to buy a fence.

## Document cheat sheet

| Document | Who provides it | When | What to do with it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer agreement | Your agent | Before touring seriously | Ask about term and cancellation |
| Loan application | You (with lender) | Day 1 of the loan | Double-check every number |
| Pre-approval letter | Lender | After application | Send to your agent |
| Loan Estimate | Lender | Within 3 business days of app | Compare across lenders |
| Purchase agreement | Your agent | When you make an offer | Make sure you know the deadlines |
| Earnest money receipt | Title/escrow | When depositing | Keep it — it's your credit at closing |
| Title commitment | Title company | Days after contract | Ask your agent about surprises |
| Inspection report | Inspector | Inspection day | Negotiate or decide within contingency |
| Appraisal report | Appraiser (via lender) | 1–3 weeks in | React if value < price |
| Closing Disclosure | Lender | 3+ days before closing | Compare vs. Loan Estimate, then confirm wiring |
| Note & mortgage | Lender | Closing day | Sign carefully, keep copies |

When in doubt: **read it before you sign it, and ask the person who gave it to you to explain the parts you don't understand.** "I don't know what this means" is a completely reasonable thing for a first-time buyer to say out loud.
