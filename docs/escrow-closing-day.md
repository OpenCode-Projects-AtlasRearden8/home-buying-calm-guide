# Escrow & Closing Day, Step by Step

The last month of a home purchase is mostly waiting punctuated by deadlines. Here is the whole arc — escrow, closing week, and closing day — in chronological order, with a **closing-day grid** at the end so the actual signature day feels scheduled rather than chaotic.

*Education only. Escalate specifics to your agent, lender, and title/escrow company as they come up; each transaction and state varies.*

## What "escrow" actually means

Escrow is a neutral third party (the escrow company, title company, or attorney depending on your region) that holds **money and documents** and releases them only when all conditions are met. Think of it as a referee that everyone hands their stakes to.

For the buyer, "in escrow" practically means:

1. Your earnest money is deposited **nowhere near the seller**. It sits with escrow.
2. Everyone is working toward closing while contingencies (inspection, financing, appraisal, title) get satisfied.
3. If the deal falls apart under a valid contingency, escrow returns your money per the contract's terms.

The escrow period is commonly 30–45 days, though it can be shorter with cash or a fast lender.

## The escrow timeline, week by week

### Week 1: Set everything in motion
- Earnest money deposited with escrow (watch for the receipt).
- Inspection scheduled and done; report received.
- Loan Estimate events: you should be watching for your lender's file requests within the first few days and returning every document **the same day you can**, because delays on your end push your closing date.
- Homeowner's insurance: start shopping **now**; you'll need an insurance binder ready before closing.

### Week 2–3: Verification and appraisal happen
- The lender's underwriting team verifies income, assets, and the title work.
- The appraiser visits and files the report.
- Respond to every lender phone call or document request the same day. Underwriting is a treadmill: it only moves when you run.
- Title work continues; if a surprise lien or name mismatch surfaces, it's usually fixable — but only with time.

### Week 3–4: The "clear to close" region
- You'll hear the phrase **"cleared to close"** — underwriting is done, and the lender schedules the final numbers.
- The lender issues your **Closing Disclosure (CD) at least 3 business days before closing**. This is your review window, and it's federally protected time, not a formality.

### Closing week: proofread like a novelist
- Compare the CD against your **Loan Estimate, section by section**: rate, term, monthly payment, cash to close, and each fee. Question every change — you have three business days to ask.
- **Confirm the wire instructions by phone**, calling a number you looked up independently (not one from the email, because wire-fraud emails targeting homebuyers are a real and common scam pattern; the "confirm by phone" rule is the standard defense).
- Ask the escrow agent: "What exactly do I need to bring, and how much?"

## Closing day, step by step

The day itself is usually 30–90 minutes of signing, at the escrow office, title office, or attorney's office. Here is what happens, in order:

1. **Arrive with your ID** (usually a driver's license; some states require two forms) and a cashier's check or your confirmed wire receipt.
2. **The closing agent reviews the settlement statement** — the final money in/money out ledger — with you.
3. You sign the **promissory note** (your promise to repay) and the **mortgage/deed of trust** (the lender's security interest). Read carefully — this is the paperwork your last few weeks paid for.
4. You sign the remaining items: the Closing Disclosure acknowledgment, title documents, escrow instructions, and any state-specific forms. Take your time — this paperwork took weeks to earn.
5. You pay the **cash to close** (if not already wired).
6. You receive keys — in some states immediately, in others only after the loan **funds** and the deed **records**. Ask your closing agent which applies, so you're not surprised standing in an empty driveway.

## The Closing-Day Grid

A one-page cheat sheet of **who does what** on closing day. Print or save this; bring it with you.

| ✓ | Who | What they do | Your move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ☐ | You (buyer) | Bring ID, cashier's check or wire confirmation, and patience | Bring a pen and read every page |
| ☐ | You (buyer) | Sign the promissory note | Verify rate, term, and amount match your CD |
| ☐ | You (buyer) | Sign the mortgage / deed of trust | Confirm the property address and escrow (tax/insurance) setup |
| ☐ | You (buyer) | Pay cash to close (if not wired) | Match the settlement statement exactly to the cent |
| ☐ | Lender rep (often remote) | Funds the loan by wire to escrow | Ask when funding confirms |
| ☐ | Closing / escrow agent | Walks through the settlement statement | Ask about anything unfamiliar |
| ☐ | Closing / escrow agent | Collects and notarizes signatures | Bring ID the agent expects |
| ☐ | Title agent | Issues title policies after recording | Confirm who pays for the owner's policy per your contract |
| ☐ | Seller | Signs the deed transferring ownership | Confirm the deed is correct before recording |
| ☐ | County recorder | Records the deed and mortgage | Recording confirms legal ownership |
| ☐ | Your real estate agent | Hands over keys / lockbox codes, per the contract | Ask what's included (remotes, codes, manuals) |

## After closing: the first 72 hours

- **File your signed documents** somewhere safe — the note, mortgage, CD, deed copy once recorded, and inspection report.
- **Set up the mortgage payment** on or before the first due date. First-time buyers commonly miss the first payment simply because it was never on autopay.
- **Save your Closing Disclosure** — you'll need it for tax season and, if relevant, for a future refinance or sale.
- **Change locks** if recommended, and set up utilities if you didn't already.
- **Start your Year One repair fund** the same week. [Budgeting for Year One](budget-year-one.html) covers the math.

## Calm-over-panic rules for the last month

1. **The 3-day CD review window is yours.** Use it. No one is rushing you legally.
2. **Verify wires by phone, always.** Wire fraud is the #1 closing-week crime, and it targets first-time buyers specifically because you've never done this before.
3. **No new debt, no job changes, no big deposits without paperwork.** Lenders re-verify right before closing. Transparency beats surprise: if a relative gifts you cash while you're in escrow, tell the lender and provide a gift letter.
4. **Ask your lender for the "clear to close" date in writing** as soon as underwriting finishes, so you're scheduling movers with facts, not vibes.
5. **If anything changes mid-escrow** (rate lock expiry, appraisal gap, title surprise), it's nearly always solvable if the parties hear about it early. Dead silence is the only thing that reliably escalates small problems.
