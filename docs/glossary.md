# Glossary A–Z

Every term in the home-buying universe you're likely to meet, in roughly one or two friendly sentences each.

*Education only.*

## A

- **Amortization** — The schedule by which your loan balance shrinks; early payments are mostly interest, late ones mostly principal.
- **Appraisal** — A licensed professional's opinion of what the house is worth, ordered by your lender to justify the loan. See [Inspection vs. Appraisal](inspection-vs-appraisal.html).
- **Appraisal contingency** — The contract clause letting you renegotiate or exit if the appraised value comes in below your price.
- **ARM (adjustable-rate mortgage)** — A loan whose rate changes on a schedule after an initial fixed period.
- **Assessed value** — What the county says your house is worth, *for property-tax purposes* (often different from market value).

## B

- **Balloon payment** — A large final payment on certain loans; rare on standard mortgages, critical to reject if you meet one.
- **Basis point** — 1/100th of a percent, the unit lenders quote rate differences in (25 basis points = 0.25%).
- **Buyer representation agreement** — The contract binding you to your buyer's agent for a set term.

## C

- **Cash to close** — The total money you bring on closing day: down payment + closing costs + prepaids − credits.
- **Clear to close** — Lender-speak for "underwriting is done." The green light to schedule signing.
- **Closing costs** — The fees (lender, title, county, insurance prepaids) due at closing, usually 2–5% of the price.
- **Closing Disclosure (CD)** — The final five-page, legally binding statement of your loan and fees, delivered at least three business days before closing.
- **Comps (comparables)** — Recent sales of similar nearby homes; the backbone of both appraisals and market pricing.
- **Contingency** — A written condition ("IF this fails, I can exit with my money") such as inspection, appraisal, or financing.
- **Conventional loan** — A mortgage not insured by a government agency; the most common type.
- **Crawlspace** — A shallow, unfinished space under some houses. Inspectors check it for moisture, because water never forgets.

## D

- **DTI (debt-to-income ratio)** — Your total monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income; a core lending metric.
- **Deed** — The document that actually conveys ownership, recorded with the county.
- **Deed of trust** — In some states, the security instrument that serves the same role as a mortgage (lender's right to foreclose).
- **Down payment** — The portion of the price you pay in cash, as distinct from closing costs.
- **Due diligence** — The inspection-period umbrella term for "checking things out before committing."

## E

- **Earnest money** — Good-faith deposit (often 1–3% of price), held in escrow and credited to you at closing.
- **Easement** — A recorded right for someone else (utility company, neighbor) to use part of your property for a purpose.
- **Encumbrance** — Any claim or restriction on the property — liens, easements, covenants.
- **Escrow** — Money and documents held by a neutral third party until all the deal's conditions are met. Also refers to the monthly collection of taxes/insurance within your mortgage payment ("escrow account").
- **Escrow account** — The sub-account your servicer uses to pay property taxes and insurance on your behalf each year.

## F

- **FHA loan** — A government-insured loan with lower down payment minimums; often used by first-time buyers.
- **Fixed-rate mortgage** — Rate never changes for the life of the loan.
- **Forbearance** — A temporary pause or reduction of mortgage payments during hardship (calls for documentation, not fear).
- **Foreclosure** — The legal process by which a lender takes the property when the mortgage isn't repaid.

## G

- **Gift letter** — A short signed statement that money from a relative is a gift, not a loan (needed whenever a relative helps your down payment).
- **GFCI outlet** — A shock-proof outlet near water. Cheap, easy to test with an outlet tester, and a thing inspectors always note.

## H

- **Home inspection** — A professional walk-through identifying the house's condition and defects, done for you. See [Touring With a Checklist](touring-checklist.html).
- **Homeowner's insurance** — The policy covering your home's structure and belongings; lenders require it before closing.
- **HOA (homeowners association)** — An organization (with dues and rules) for some condo/townhouse/planned-communities.

## I

- **Intent to proceed** — Your instruction to a lender to move forward with a specific Loan Estimate. No fees before this.
- **Interest rate** — The percentage the lender charges you annually on the loan balance.

## L

- **Loan Estimate (LE)** — A standard three-page summary of a loan offer, used to compare lenders.
- **Lock (rate lock)** — A lender's commitment to honor a quoted rate for a set period (often 30–60 days).
- **Lien** — A legal claim on the property for an unpaid debt; liens generally must be released at closing.

## M

- **Mortgage** — The loan, plus the security instrument recorded against the property in states that use "mortgages" rather than deeds of trust.
- **Mortgage insurance (PMI/MIP)** — Insurance protecting the *lender* when a buyer's down payment is under roughly 20%; it adds monthly cost for many buyers.

## N

- **Note (promissory note)** — Your signed promise to repay the loan: amount, rate, term.

## O

- **Owner's title policy** — Title insurance protecting *you* against ownership-history problems; optional but widely recommended.

## P

- **PITI** — Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance — the complete monthly payment in one acronym.
- **PMI** — Private mortgage insurance; see "Mortgage insurance."
- **Points** — Prepaid interest: paying more upfront for a lower rate; worth comparing side by side.
- **Pre-approval** — A lender's conditional commitment after checking your real documents. See [Pre-approval Without Panic](pre-approval.html).
- **Pre-qualification** — A self-reported, unofficial estimate of what a lender might approve.
- **Prepaids** — Upfront payments at closing (property taxes, insurance) that build your escrow account.
- **Purchase agreement (PSA)** — The binding contract for the sale: price, dates, contingencies.

## R

- **Recording** — The county's official registration of your deed and mortgage.
- **Refinance** — Replacing your current mortgage with a new one, for a better rate or different term.

## S

- **Seller's disclosure** — A form on which the seller reports known issues with the house; still worth a professional inspection.
- **Settlement statement** — The complete ledger of who pays whom; full versions live on your Closing Disclosure.
- **Survey** — A drawn map of property boundaries and any surrounding structures' relation to them.

## T

- **Title** — Legal ownership of the property.
- **Title commitment** — The title company's report of ownership history, liens, easements, and requirements to close.
- **Title insurance** — Coverage against undiscovered problems with the property's ownership history.
- **Title search** — The process of reviewing records to confirm the seller can actually sell you the house.

## U–Z

- **Underwriting** — The lender's verification of you, the property, and all the paperwork; leads to "clear to close."
- **VA loan** — A government-backed loan for eligible veterans and service members.
- **Walk-through** — The final pre-closing visit to confirm the house is in the agreed condition.
- **Wire instructions** — The bank details for sending your cash to close. Always verify by phone.

Still confused by a word your agent used? Ask them to use the two-sentence version. Good agents do this reflexively; great agents enjoy it.
