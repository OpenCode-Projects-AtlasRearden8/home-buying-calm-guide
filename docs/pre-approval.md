# Pre-approval Without Panic

Pre-approval is where most first-time buyers lose sleep. It doesn't have to be that way. Here's what actually happens, in order, with tips for keeping your blood pressure flat the whole way through.

*Education only. This is a walkthrough of the process, not advice about what loan type or amount is right for you.*

## Pre-qualification vs. pre-approval vs. approval

Three words that sound like synonyms but aren't:

| Term | What it is | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-qualification | A 10-minute estimate based on **numbers you self-report** | Almost nothing. It's a rough sketch. |
| Pre-approval | A lender checks your actual documents (pay stubs, bank statements, credit) and issues a letter | You're a credible buyer, roughly up to $X |
| Approval (underwritten) | The lender has fully underwritten the loan, pending only a property | The strongest signal short of cash |

Most buyers only need **pre-approval**. Some competitive markets reward "underwritten" or "verified" pre-approvals — ask your lender if they offer one.

## What you'll need to gather

1. **Income:** Last 2 pay stubs, last 2 years of W-2s (or 1099s/tax returns if self-employed).
2. **Assets:** Last 2 months of statements for every financial account — checking, savings, brokerage, retirement.
3. **Identity:** Driver's license or ID, Social Security number (they pull your credit for you).
4. **Debts:** They'll see most of these on your credit report; be ready to explain anything unusual (student loans in forbearance, a paid-off car that still shows, etc.).
5. **Any money gifts:** If a relative is helping with the down payment, most lenders need a "gift letter" — a short signed note saying it's a gift, not a loan. Get this early.

**Helpful tip:** Write one plain-English note to yourself explaining anything unusual in your finances ("car paid off in March, still shows on credit"; "second job is travel nursing, contract renews quarterly"). Lenders love documentation; a note from you helps them write fewer emails.

## The credit question (the scary part, briefly)

Lenders "pull" your credit, which is a hard inquiry. Yes, it temporarily dings your score a few points.

Two genuinely useful facts:

- **Rate shopping is safe:** Multiple mortgage inquiries within a roughly 45-day window are treated as **one** inquiry for scoring purposes. You do not need to be afraid to compare lenders.
- **Don't add new debt during the process:** New car loan, new credit card, furniture financing for the house you don't have yet — all of these change your debt-to-income ratio, and lenders check again just before closing. New debt right before closing is one of the most common ways people unwind their own deal.

## What the lender is calculating

Two numbers drive everything:

- **DTI (debt-to-income ratio):** Your total monthly debt payments (including the future house payment) divided by your gross monthly income. Under roughly 43% is typical for many conventional loans; lower is better.
- **Cash to close:** Down payment + closing costs (often 2–5% of price) + prepaid taxes/insurance, minus your earnest money credit.

You do not need to know the exact math. What you *do* need to know is what monthly payment feels comfortable to **you**, including taxes, insurance, HOA if any, and — where applicable — PMI. That number is your north star; we'll budget for it in [Budgeting for Year One](budget-year-one.html).

## Your six-step, no-panic path

1. **Gather documents first** (list above). This is 80% of the work and 100% of the cure for panic.
2. **Pick 2–3 different kinds of lenders to compare:** a big national bank, a local credit union or mortgage broker, and one online lender.
3. **Apply to all of them within a few days** of each other, so the rate-shopping window applies and the credit inquiries count once.
4. **Compare Loan Estimates side by side:** rate on page 1, origination charges on page 2, cash to close on page 3. A 0.25% rate difference on a 30-year loan is a five-figure difference over the life of the loan — fees matter as much as the headline rate.
5. **Choose one, send an "intent to proceed,"** and keep the others' LEs in your file. You can still re-quote later if the market moves.
6. **Then stop touching your credit.** Freeze all applications for anything — cards, cars, furniture financing — until the keys are in your hand.

## Red flags while shopping

- Pressure to sign an intent to proceed on the first call.
- "Hold this rate until 5pm today," with no paperwork to confirm it.
- Fees that don't appear on the Loan Estimate, only in emails or phone calls.
- Requests for wire transfers to personal accounts, or anything but the lender's official process.

*Nothing here is advice about what loan or amount suits you — that's your call with your lender and, if you wish, a fee-only advisor who doesn't earn commissions on your decision.*

## Sanity checklist before you call the first lender

- [ ] 2 pay stubs
- [ ] 2 years of W-2s or returns
- [ ] 2 months of bank statements (all accounts)
- [ ] ID
- [ ] A written guess of the monthly payment you want
- [ ] The names of 2–3 lenders to compare
- [ ] A calendar block of 2 quiet hours for the paperwork

That's it. Pre-approval done calmly is just paperwork done in the right order.
